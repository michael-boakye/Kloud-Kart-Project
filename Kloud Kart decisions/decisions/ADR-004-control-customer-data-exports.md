# ADR-004: Introduce Formal Controls for Customer Data Exports

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decision owner:** Nana Adjei, Head of Operations

## Context

Marketing exports customer lists containing approximately 300,000 records. The company does not currently track how many copies exist, where they are stored or who receives them. This creates difficulty in controlling customer information and responding to deletion requests.

## Decision

Kloud Kart will introduce a controlled process for customer data exports, including named responsibility, authorised access, export records, storage controls and a documented process for handling deletion requests.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Continue allowing departments to manage their own customer-list copies

- **How it would work:** Marketing and other departments would continue exporting and storing customer information according to their individual working practices.
- **Why it is attractive:** It allows departments to work independently without introducing additional administrative steps.
- **Why rejected:** The company would continue to lack a reliable record of where customer information is held and who has received it. This makes it harder to demonstrate responsible handling of personal information under Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843).

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** Better accountability, clearer access control and evidence to support customer information requests and regulatory responses.
- **What this costs:** Employees will need to follow an export and access process. The company will also need to maintain records and assign responsibility for customer information.
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** Controls that are too restrictive could slow legitimate marketing activities, while weak controls could leave customer information exposed.
- **What would make me revisit this:** Changes in the company's data use, the introduction of new systems or evidence that the controls are either ineffective or unnecessarily restrictive.

## What this survives

As the customer base grows and the company expands into Nigeria, customer information will remain subject to an identifiable process rather than being managed through untracked copies. The process will also provide evidence to support the assignment's 90-day response requirement for a Data Protection Commission complaint.
